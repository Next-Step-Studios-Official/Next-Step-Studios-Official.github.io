> **Draft.** This policy sets out an intended approach. It is not a statement that any right has been registered, asserted, or enforced. It requires legal review, particularly because enforcement by a minor raises capacity questions.

---

## 1. Purpose

The project is proprietary and its work is valuable. This policy records how the studio intends to respond to infringement: proportionately, with evidence, and without treating ordinary fandom as a threat.

## 2. Rights Held

| Right | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Copyright in original code, assets, and writing | Held automatically by Candler Shaw as author. No registration claimed. |
| Copyright registration | None |
| Trademark in "Places Left Behind" | None registered. The name is used as a project title. |
| Trademark in "Next Step Studios" | None registered. Used as a public-facing name. |
| Database or design rights | Not applicable to the current material |
| Patents | None |

Because no trademark is registered, protection of the names relies on ordinary law against misleading use, not on a registered right.

## 3. Enforcement Principles

1. **Proportionality.** Response matches the harm. A small fan page is not treated like a commercial clone.
2. **Evidence first.** No action without an identified right and a documented instance of the conduct.
3. **Contact before escalation.** A direct message resolves most issues.
4. **Ordinary fandom is not a target.** Discussion, criticism, guides, videos, screenshots, and fan art are not enforcement targets.
5. **No over-claiming.** The studio does not assert rights it does not hold, including rights in third-party material or in unprotectable ideas.
6. **Consistency.** Similar situations are handled similarly, so the position is predictable.
7. **Record keeping.** Every action and its basis is recorded.

## 4. What Is Treated as Infringement

| Conduct | Concern level |
| --- | --- |
| Redistribution of game builds or installation files | High |
| Sale of the game, assets, or derivative works | High |
| Including the game's assets in another commercial product | High |
| Publishing a modified build as a "fixed" or "complete" version | High |
| Claiming authorship of the game or its assets | Medium to high |
| Using the game's name or logo to imply official status | Medium |
| Republishing the game's documentation or licensing documents | Medium |
| Extracting assets for use in another project | Medium |
| Re-uploading a Mod without the author's permission | Medium — handled under modding terms, and the Mod author is the rights holder |
| Screenshots, videos, criticism, guides, fan art | Not a concern |

The distinction matters: a re-uploaded Mod infringes the *Mod author's* rights, not primarily the studio's. The studio should support the author rather than act as though it owns the Mod.

## 5. Response Ladder

1. **Assess.** Identify the right, the material, the scale, and whether the conduct is commercial or personal.
2. **Document.** Capture evidence with dates, URLs, and screenshots.
3. **Contact.** Send a factual message describing the concern and requesting a change. Most matters end here.
4. **Platform notice.** Where the material is hosted on a service, use the service's complaint process. See the [Copyright and Takedown Policy](/docs/places-left-behind/copyright-and-takedown).
5. **Formal demand.** A letter from a lawyer, if the matter is significant and contact has failed.
6. **Legal action.** Only with a lawyer, and only where the harm justifies the cost and the capacity position is settled.

## 6. What the Studio Will Not Do

- Send automated or mass notices without review.
- Threaten action it does not intend to take.
- Claim ownership of third-party material.
- Target criticism, negative reviews, or critical videos.
- Target a minor without involving a guardian and legal advice.
- Assert a trademark right it has not registered as though it were registered.

## 7. Trademark and Naming

1. The studio has no registered trademark. Any statement implying registration would be false.
2. The studio may ask that its names not be used in a way that suggests endorsement, affiliation, or official status.
3. Descriptive and nominative uses — referring to the game by name in a review, a guide, or a Mod page — are expected and accepted.
4. Whether to seek trademark protection is an open decision.

## 8. Enforcement Against Minors

The studio's operator is currently a minor, and some infringers may also be minors. The intended approach:

1. Involve a parent or guardian before taking formal steps against a minor.
2. Prefer a request to remove over a formal demand.
3. Involve a lawyer before any step with legal consequences.
4. Record the reasoning.

## 9. Evidence and Records

For each matter, keep:

- the right relied on and the material said to infringe;
- dates, URLs, and screenshots;
- the communications sent and received;
- the action taken and its outcome;
- any legal advice received, kept separately and confidentially.

## 10. Costs and Escalation

Formal enforcement is expensive. Before escalating, the studio should weigh:

1. the commercial harm;
2. the likelihood of recovery;
3. the cost of action;
4. the reputational effect;
5. whether a platform process would resolve it more cheaply.

## 11. Legal Review Requirements

1. Confirm the rights actually held and how they can be evidenced without registration.
2. Confirm the position on unregistered trademark protection in the intended jurisdiction.
3. Confirm the capacity position and guardian involvement for any enforcement step.
4. Confirm the statutory requirements for notices and counter-notices.
5. Confirm the intended jurisdiction, Georgia, United States.
6. Prepare template communications with a lawyer.
