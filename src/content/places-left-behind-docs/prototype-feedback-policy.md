## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how feedback about Places Left Behind prototypes is submitted, what happens to it, and what rights the owner and the tester each have in it.

It is written so that testers can report problems freely, and so that the owner can use reports to improve the game without ambiguity about ownership or ongoing obligations.

## 2. What Counts as Feedback

- Bug reports, crash reports, and reproduction steps.
- Performance observations and hardware details relevant to a defect.
- Suggestions for gameplay, interface, atmosphere, accessibility, or content.
- Opinions, impressions, and criticism.
- Screenshots, recordings, logs, and save files shared to illustrate a problem.

Feedback does not include: prototype builds themselves, extracted game assets, third-party material the tester has no right to share, or personal data about other people.

## 3. How to Submit Feedback

1. The owner designates a feedback channel for each testing round — for example a private issue tracker, a form, or a direct message.
2. Reports should include the build identifier shown by the game or supplied with the download, the steps taken, what was expected, and what happened.
3. Screenshots and recordings are welcome when they help explain a problem.
4. Testers should not publish private download links, source code, or unreleased spoiler content in a report.
5. Security issues should be reported privately to the owner rather than publicly. See section 8.

**Current status:** no channel has been designated and no builds exist. This section describes the intended process.

## 4. Licence Granted in Feedback

By submitting Feedback, the tester grants the owner a **perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free, non-exclusive licence** to:

1. use, reproduce, store, and analyse the Feedback;
2. modify, adapt, and combine the Feedback with other material;
3. incorporate ideas and suggestions from the Feedback into the game, engine, documentation, and related products;
4. display and distribute the Feedback internally, and externally where the tester has agreed or where it has been de-identified.

This licence includes the right to sublicense these rights to contractors and collaborators working on the project, to the extent needed to develop it.

**What the licence does not do:**

- It does not transfer ownership of the tester's original expression. The tester keeps their copyright in what they wrote, drew, or recorded.
- It does not permit the owner to sell the tester's feedback as a standalone product, or to republish it as the tester's work.
- It does not grant the owner rights in third-party material the tester included without permission, and the tester must not include such material.

**No obligation:** the owner is not obliged to use any Feedback, to credit it, to pay for it, or to respond to it. There is no confidentiality obligation on the owner in respect of unsolicited ideas, and no obligation to keep a submission confidential unless a separate written arrangement says so.

## 5. Ideas and Feature Suggestions

Game development frequently produces ideas independently of any suggestion. A tester who suggests a feature does not acquire any ownership of the game, any right to compensation, or any claim over a similar feature the owner later develops, whether or not the suggestion influenced it.

If a tester believes a specific contribution deserves credit or an arrangement beyond this policy, that must be agreed in writing before the contribution is made.

## 6. Attribution and Credits

- The owner may thank testers by name in credits, patch notes, or on the studio's website.
- Testers may ask to be credited under a preferred name or to be omitted.
- A credit is a courtesy. It is not a licence, not a payment, and not an admission of joint authorship.

## 7. What the Owner Will Do With Feedback

1. Review reports and prioritise them by severity and usefulness.
2. Fix defects at the owner's discretion; not every report will be acted on.
3. Keep reports for as long as they are useful for development, then delete them.
4. Avoid publishing a tester's identity or contact details without permission.
5. Avoid publishing a report in a form that identifies the tester, unless the tester agrees.

Detailed retention and deletion rules are in [`../Privacy/Game-Data-Policy.md`](/docs/places-left-behind/game-data-policy). Note that no feedback is currently stored because no programme exists.

## 8. Security Issues

A security issue in the prototype — for example a crash reachable from a malformed file, or a path traversal in a loader — should be reported privately to the owner before any public disclosure. The owner will aim to acknowledge a report and to discuss a reasonable disclosure timeline. No formal security policy or response commitment is in force; the [Community Reporting Policy](/docs/places-left-behind/community-reporting-policy) draft describes the intended process.

## 9. Conduct

Feedback channels must not be used for harassment, spam, or the distribution of malware, pirated material, or other people's personal data. The owner may remove such content and end access.

## 10. No Warranty or Support Commitment

Feedback does not create a support contract. There is no service level, no guaranteed response time, and no commitment to fix any defect or to implement any suggestion.

## 11. Changes and Legal Review

- The owner may revise this policy for future testing rounds. A revision does not retroactively change the terms applying to feedback already submitted, unless the tester accepts it.
- The intended jurisdiction is Georgia, United States, subject to legal review and confirmation.
- The owner is currently under 18, so capacity and guardian involvement should be reviewed before this policy is used with testers, especially where testers are also minors.
- No attorney has reviewed this policy.

## 12. Open Items Requiring Confirmation

- Which channel will be used, and whether it will be private or semi-public.
- Whether click-through acceptance of this policy will be required before a tester can submit.
- Whether feedback containing personal data will be accepted, and how it will be handled.
- Whether a formal security disclosure policy should be adopted before any build is shared.
