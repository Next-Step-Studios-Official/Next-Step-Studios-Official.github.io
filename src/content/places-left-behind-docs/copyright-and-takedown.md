## 1. Purpose

This policy has two sides:

1. **Inbound:** how a third party can report material that infringes their copyright, and how such a report is handled.
2. **Outbound:** how the studio handles infringement of its own work.

Copyright in Places Left Behind is owned by **Candler Shaw**, an individual. Next Step Studios is a public-facing name, not a legal entity, and does not own copyright independently.

## 2. Current Status

- There is no public-facing takedown contact and no designated agent.
- No community platform or content host operated by the studio exists.
- No complaint has been received or handled.
- Under United States law, certain safe-harbour protections for user-generated content depend on designating an agent with the Copyright Office. **No such designation has been made.** This must be resolved before the studio hosts third-party content.

## 3. Reporting Infringement to the Studio

A copyright owner, or their authorised representative, may report material they believe infringes their rights.

A complete report should include:

1. **Identification of the work** — the copyrighted work claimed to be infringed, or a representative list if several works are covered.
2. **Identification of the material** — enough detail to locate the allegedly infringing material, including a URL or exact location.
3. **Contact details** — name, address, telephone number, and email address of the reporting party.
4. **Good faith statement** — a statement that the reporting party believes in good faith that the use is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
5. **Accuracy statement** — a statement that the information in the notice is accurate, and that the reporting party is the copyright owner or is authorised to act on the owner's behalf, made under penalty of perjury.
6. **Signature** — a physical or electronic signature of the copyright owner or authorised representative.

Reports should be sent to the contact address published with the game or on the studio's website. No postal address exists to publish, and none should be invented.

## 4. What Happens Next

The intended process:

1. **Acknowledge.** Confirm receipt where a valid contact address is provided.
2. **Assess.** Check whether the report is complete and whether the material is within the studio's control.
3. **Act.** If the report is complete and the claim appears credible, remove or disable access to the material, or ask the uploader to act.
4. **Notify.** Tell the uploader that material was removed, unless doing so would be inappropriate.
5. **Record.** Keep a record of the report, the action, and the date.
6. **Counter-notice.** Explain to the uploader how to respond if they believe the removal was mistaken.

No timelines are promised. Any statutory deadlines that apply must be identified during legal review.

## 5. Counter-Notice

Where material was removed from a space the studio controls, the uploader may submit a counter-notice stating that the material was removed by mistake or misidentification, with their contact details, consent to jurisdiction, and signature.

The intended handling:

1. Review the counter-notice for completeness.
2. Forward it to the original reporting party where appropriate.
3. Restore the material if the reporting party does not pursue the matter within the applicable period.
4. Keep a record of the outcome.

This section must be checked against the statutory counter-notice requirements that apply before it is used.

## 6. Repeat Infringers

Where the studio hosts user content, it intends to terminate access for users who repeatedly infringe. No such system exists, and no threshold has been set.

## 7. Bad-Faith Reports

A report made knowing that the material is not infringing may expose the reporting party to liability under applicable law. The studio may decline reports that appear abusive, and may restrict repeat bad-faith reporters from using its reporting process.

## 8. The Studio's Own Rights

The studio's material is proprietary. Where the studio believes its copyright is infringed, the intended approach is:

1. **Verify.** Confirm the studio's ownership and the specific material infringed.
2. **Assess.** Consider whether the use is permitted by law, by a licence, or by the studio's own community terms.
3. **Contact first.** Where practical, contact the person responsible before escalating.
4. **Use platform processes.** Send a platform notice where the material is hosted on a service with a complaint process.
5. **Escalate only when warranted.** Legal action is a last resort, and requires a lawyer.

The fuller posture is in the [Intellectual Property Enforcement Policy](/docs/places-left-behind/ip-enforcement-policy).

## 9. What the Studio Does Not Claim

- The studio does not claim ownership of third-party material included under its own licence.
- The studio does not claim that a name, location, or general idea is protected by copyright.
- The studio has no registered copyright and no registered trademark.
- The studio does not assert that ideas, mechanics, or genres are owned.

## 10. Legal Review Requirements

1. Confirm the statutory notice and counter-notice requirements that apply.
2. Confirm whether an agent must be designated, and how, before hosting user content.
3. Confirm the repeat-infringer policy needed for any safe harbour.
4. Confirm how a takedown contact is published without a business address.
5. Confirm the capacity position, given the owner is under 18, and whether a guardian must act.
6. Confirm the intended jurisdiction, Georgia, United States.
7. Prepare a template notice and counter-notice with a lawyer.
