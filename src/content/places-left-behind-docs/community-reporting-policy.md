## 1. Purpose

A community space needs a way to report problems that is clear, safe for the reporter, and fair to the person reported.

This policy describes that process. It is written before any space exists so that moderation starts with a considered approach rather than being improvised under pressure.

## 2. What Can Be Reported

1. **Content problems** — infringing material, malware, stolen assets, prohibited content.
2. **Conduct problems** — harassment, threats, hate speech, doxxing, impersonation.
3. **Safety problems** — content involving minors, self-harm, or credible threats of violence.
4. **Security problems** — a vulnerability in the game, a Mod, or a studio service.
5. **Spam and fraud** — fake downloads, fake official pages, scam links.
6. **Licensing problems** — a Mod that redistributes game assets, or an asset used without permission.

## 3. How to Report

1. Use the reporting mechanism provided by the platform hosting the space, where one exists.
2. Otherwise contact the studio through the published contact route.
3. Include what happened, where, when, and any links or screenshots.
4. Do not repost the offending material as part of the report if it is illegal or harmful; describe it and provide a link instead.

No reporting channel is currently published.

## 4. Safety-First Reports

Some reports need priority:

- threats of violence;
- content sexualising minors;
- credible self-harm risk;
- doxxing or exposure of private information.

The intended handling: act first to reduce harm — remove or hide the material — then investigate. Where there is an immediate risk to a person, contact emergency services rather than relying on the studio's process.

## 5. What the Studio Would Do

1. **Acknowledge** where practical.
2. **Assess** the report against this policy and the community terms.
3. **Act** — remove content, warn, restrict, or take no action.
4. **Explain** the outcome to the reporter in general terms, without disclosing another person's private information.
5. **Record** the report and the action.
6. **Review** patterns, so repeated problems are addressed at the source.

No response time is promised. A small studio cannot offer a service level.

## 6. Fairness to the Person Reported

1. A report is a claim, not a finding.
2. Where practical, the person reported is told what the concern is and given a chance to respond.
3. Action should be proportionate: removal of a specific item before removal of a person.
4. Persistent or serious breaches may lead to removal from studio-operated spaces.
5. A person may appeal a decision by replying to the decision notice.

## 7. Anonymity and Reporter Privacy

1. A reporter's identity is not published.
2. Where a report is shared with a platform or a lawyer, the reporter's identity is shared only when necessary.
3. A reporter may ask to remain anonymous. An anonymous report may be harder to act on, and the studio will say so.
4. A reporter who acts in bad faith may be restricted from the reporting process.

## 8. Security Reports

A security issue should be reported privately, not publicly.

1. Describe the issue and how to reproduce it.
2. Allow reasonable time for a fix before public disclosure.
3. Do not access other people's data, disrupt services, or publish exploit code.
4. The studio will aim to acknowledge and to discuss a timeline, but makes no formal commitment today.

A formal security policy and disclosure timeline have not been adopted.

## 9. Copyright Reports

Copyright complaints follow a separate process with specific statutory requirements. See the [Copyright and Takedown Policy](/docs/places-left-behind/copyright-and-takedown). Do not send a copyright notice through a general reporting channel, because it may not contain the information needed to act.

## 10. Moderation Standards

Where the studio operates a space, moderators would be expected to:

1. apply the published rules consistently;
2. explain decisions;
3. avoid acting on personal animosity;
4. escalate anything involving legal risk;
5. keep records.

The expectation of conduct is in the [Community Content Policy](/docs/places-left-behind/community-content-policy).

## 11. Record Keeping and Privacy

1. Records of reports should be kept only as long as needed to handle the matter and to identify patterns.
2. Records should not include more personal data than necessary.
3. Records should be stored in access-controlled accounts, not in the repository.
4. Data handling is described in [`../Privacy/Game-Data-Policy.md`](/docs/places-left-behind/game-data-policy).

## 12. What This Policy Does Not Do

- It does not create a moderation service that currently exists.
- It does not promise a response time or an outcome.
- It does not make the studio responsible for content on platforms it does not operate.
- It does not override a platform's own rules, which apply in addition.

## 13. Open Items

1. Which community spaces will exist, and on which platforms.
2. Which reporting mechanism will be used.
3. Who moderates, and what authority they have.
4. Whether an appeal process needs a second reviewer.
5. Whether a formal security disclosure policy will be adopted.
6. Legal review of the takedown and moderation provisions.
7. Confirmation of the intended jurisdiction, Georgia, United States.
