## 1. Purpose and Principles

Places Left Behind is a single-player exploration game about forgotten places and the stories left in them. A community that maps, writes, films, and discusses those places is a natural extension of that idea.

The principles in this policy:

1. **Community creators keep what they make.** The studio does not claim ownership of community creations.
2. **The studio's own work stays the studio's.** Community content must not redistribute the game's proprietary files.
3. **Permission is specific.** Where the studio needs to host, display, or distribute community content, it asks for exactly that, and nothing broader.
4. **Removal is possible.** The studio can remove infringing or malicious content from spaces it controls.
5. **Ordinary fandom is welcome.** Discussion, criticism, guides, videos, and fan art do not need permission.

## 3. Ownership

1. **You own your original community content.** The studio does not acquire ownership of your art, writing, video, guide, translation, or Mod by virtue of it existing or being discussed.
2. **The studio's content remains the studio's.** The game, its assets, and its writing remain owned by Candler Shaw.
3. **No implied assignment.** Posting content in a space the studio operates does not transfer ownership, unless separate submission terms explicitly say otherwise and you accept them.
4. **Credit stays with the creator.** Where the studio features community content, it credits the creator as agreed.

## 4. What the Studio May Do Without Asking

The studio may, without a separate licence:

1. read, watch, and respond to public community content;
2. link to public community content;
3. quote short excerpts of public commentary in a way that is fair and attributed;
4. reference the existence of a Mod, guide, or video in patch notes or announcements, with attribution.

The studio may not, without permission:

1. reupload your content in full;
2. use your art or Mod assets inside the game;
3. sell or license your content;
4. present your work as the studio's own;
5. use your content in paid advertising without a separate agreement.

## 5. Featuring and Showcasing

Where the studio wants to feature community content — for example a Mod spotlight or a screenshot showcase — it will:

1. ask the creator for permission, in writing where practical;
2. state where and how the content will appear;
3. credit the creator by the name they choose;
4. avoid altering the content beyond cropping or formatting for presentation;
5. stop using it if the creator withdraws permission for future use.

A feature is not an endorsement of everything the creator makes, and is not an official seal of approval.

## 6. Translations and Localisation

1. A fan translation of game text is a derivative work of the studio's writing. It requires permission to be distributed, and the studio may grant it case by case.
2. Where permission is granted, the translation must be distributed as a Mod, not as a replacement build of the game.
3. The translator is credited for the translation work, and the studio retains the underlying text.
4. Unofficial translations must be labelled as unofficial.

## 7. Prohibited Content

Content that does any of the following is not permitted in spaces the studio controls, and may be removed:

1. redistributes the game's proprietary files or assets;
2. distributes the game itself, or a modified build that bypasses ownership checks;
3. contains malware, credential theft, or hidden downloads;
4. infringes copyright, trademark, or other rights;
5. publishes another person's private information;
6. harasses, threatens, or defames a person;
7. sexualises minors, or depicts them inappropriately in any way;
8. promotes hatred against a group;
9. impersonates the studio, its developer, or another community member;
10. is fraudulent — for example fake download links, fake official status, or fake endorsements.

## 8. Conduct in Studio-Operated Spaces

Where the studio operates a space — a Discord server, forum, or comment section — participants are expected to:

1. be civil;
2. keep criticism honest but directed at the work, not at the person;
3. avoid spoilers without warning;
4. respect the rules of that specific space;
5. avoid spam and self-promotion unrelated to the game.

Moderation decisions are made by the studio. The [Community Reporting Policy](/docs/places-left-behind/community-reporting-policy) draft describes how reports would be handled.

## 9. Takedowns and Removal

1. The studio may remove content from spaces it controls when the content breaches this policy.
2. A copyright owner may request removal of infringing content. The draft process is in the [Copyright and Takedown Policy](/docs/places-left-behind/copyright-and-takedown).
3. Where practical, the studio will explain a removal and what would need to change.
4. Removal from a studio-controlled space does not affect content elsewhere, which the studio does not control.
5. Removal does not transfer ownership or waive the creator's rights.

## 10. Attribution and Credits

1. The studio credits community creators where it features their work.
2. Creators may ask to be credited under a chosen name, or to be left uncredited. A credit request does not create an ownership claim over the game.
3. The studio does not have to credit content it merely links to, though it will normally name the creator.

## 11. No Warranty and No Endorsement

1. Community content is not produced, tested, or supported by the studio.
2. Featuring or linking content does not make it official, safe, or endorsed.
3. Users install Mods and follow guides at their own risk.

## 12. Minors

The game's community may include minors. The policy intends to:

1. avoid collecting personal data from community members;
2. require guardian involvement where a minor enters an agreement with the studio;
3. prohibit content that sexualises minors, absolutely and without exception.

Any community platform chosen must have its own age and safety rules, which the studio would need to follow.

## 13. Legal Review and Open Items

1. Whether featuring content creates any implied licence broader than intended.
2. Whether a submission process needs explicit acceptance terms rather than an implied licence.
3. How removal rights interact with a creator's own licence to their content.
4. Whether the studio should register a takedown contact.
5. Confirmation of the intended jurisdiction, Georgia, United States.
6. Capacity and guardian involvement, given the owner is currently under 18.
